    not reargue it. What each row adds is the instruction after:
    the base is read back, and if it is gone a psABI access is
    made and its outcome recorded.

    neighbouring registers and flags ok       destination written, r10, r11 and the carry flag intact
    no call site                     ok       1304000 reads, every one faulted and every one correct
    concurrent faults                ok       15662000 reads across 24 threads, none wrong
    cost of a handled fault          ok       2215 ns handled against 0.5 ns rewritten, 4587x

    The forms are what a compiler emits for TLS on this target.
    The read-modify-write form is in the list to be refused:
    emulating it means emulating EFLAGS, and a handler that
    gets the carry flag wrong is worse than one that declines.

    The two costs are not the same kind of number. A handled
    fault is serial by construction -- nothing overlaps with a
    kernel dispatch -- and the rewritten access is measured at
    throughput, several in flight at once. So the ratio is the
    most generous reading available to the rewriter, and the
    honest claim is the order of magnitude rather than the
    figure: three, not one.
